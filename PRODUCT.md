# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

One staff member at the CeX Eastbourne branch, standing on the shop floor with their own phone, usually with a customer waiting. They turn the screen towards the customer when showing recommendations, so anything on a recommendation screen is read by the customer too.

Customers are mostly price-led: they want a cheap phone, or a phone that does the job. Price steps that work are small (often £10–£30, up to about £60 by default).

## Product Purpose

Shelf answers "what do we have in stock right now that suits this customer?" faster than the till system or the public website can. Success is a staff member getting from a customer's opening sentence to a specific in-stock item, with a reason to buy it, in a few seconds.

It also helps the staff member offer a step up on every sale: a slightly better item for a little more money, with factual reasons the customer can see.

## Positioning

Every result is physically in stock at one branch, and every phone, tablet and computer is rated for whether it still gets software updates and how fast it is. Neither CeX's website nor its till shows that.

## Operating Context

- Used in short bursts during a conversation with a customer, one-handed, on a phone.
- Three entry points: the customer names a specific device (step up from it), the customer names a budget (match it), or the staff member just searches.
- Shelf labels behind cabinet glass can be scanned to identify an item and check its current system price.
- Sales approach confirmed by the user: show a cheaper option and what it lacks, a dearer option and what it adds, and a well-matched option in between.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stock and prices come from the search service behind uk.webuy.com. It is unofficial and can change without notice.
- Update-support dates come from endoflife.date; Apple dates are estimates.
- Speed ratings are approximate chip benchmarks. Camera and battery facts are a hand-compiled table covering common models only; when a fact is not known it is omitted, never guessed.
- Battery figures are capacity when new. Battery health of a used item is not known.
- Identical items share one product ID; per-store quantity is not available.
- Recommendation flows cover phones and tablets. Search covers everything.
- The step-up price limit defaults to about £60 and is adjustable by the user in settings.
- No backend and no accounts: preferences live on the device.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Shelf.
- Voice: plain, factual, short. Recommendation screens are customer-safe: no sales notes, no put-downs, only facts that are true of the item.

## Evidence on Hand

- Live in-stock listings, prices, grades and product photos from CeX.
- Update lifecycle data for iPhone, Samsung, Pixel, OnePlus, Motorola, Nokia, Sony, Fairphone.
- No customer reviews, sales figures or battery-health data exist here; future work must not invent them.

## Product Principles

1. Only what is on the shelf. Never recommend something the branch cannot sell today.
2. Reasons are facts. A step up is justified by updates, speed, storage, camera, battery or condition that are true of both items, or it is not suggested.
3. Small steps. Lead with the smallest worthwhile extra spend; this is not a luxury sales floor.
4. Say what the saving costs. A cheaper option is shown with what it gives up, plainly.
5. Seconds, not screens. The answer appears without typing more than a price or a model name.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Read at arm's length under shop lighting, sometimes by the customer: large prices, high contrast, status never carried by colour alone.
